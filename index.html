Mixed Media Installation, 2026

Holding You Together

In this installation, I explore how war breaks families apart through the contrast between a fractured mother and an intact infant. I sculpted the mother from stone clay and covered her torso with collected porcelain shards, while shaping and firing the baby as a smooth ceramic form. The mother’s worn face and broken body contrast with the infant resting peacefully in her arms. I placed them among bricks, broken wood, and a painted backdrop to suggest a home damaged by war. Reusing discarded porcelain, I wanted the fragments to convey both the mother’s suffering and her hope to protect her child’s future.

Projection Installation, 2026

Patterns in Motion

Inspired by questions about the boundary between motion and stillness, I created this multimedia installation to explore how surface patterns change our perception of ceramic form. Using generative AI, I animated traditional motifs and projected them onto two unglazed bisque vases and a large plate. Chrysanthemums bloom, cranes take flight, and dragon motifs transform across their surfaces. The vessels remain still, while the projected patterns continually change their appearance. By replacing permanent decoration with moving light, I wanted to give familiar cultural imagery a sense of time and movement, exploring how ceramic surfaces can carry images that are never fixed.

Site-specific Photographic Installation, 2026

A Mountain Behind Every Vase

This site-specific installation explores the environmental cost of porcelain production in Jingdezhen. After visiting 20 local handcraft workshops and industrial factories, I photographed a mountain of ceramic waste and installed large prints in the warehouse of a factory selling porcelain. I placed the images directly behind finished vases, bringing discarded fragments and polished products into the same display. From a distance, the waste appears almost like another pattern among the ceramics; closer inspection reveals a landscape of broken pieces. I wanted to draw attention to what lies beyond a vase as a commodity: the environmental cost of the industry behind it.

Recycled Ceramic Sculptures, 2026

Remembrance Art: Memories Recast in Clay

“Remembrance Art” is a public art initiative in which I transformed broken ceramic keepsakes into personal memorials. Through interviews with family, friends, and teachers, I collected objects and their stories, including my late grandmother’s bowl, a teacher’s family relic, and a lost cat’s dish. After testing more than 300 pieces, I developed a recycled clay recipe containing up to 10% pulverized porcelain. Using this material, I sculpted three memorials: Flowing Apple, Feather Bowl, and Cat’s Pawprint. By incorporating fragments of treasured objects into new forms, I wanted to preserve a physical connection to what had been lost and explore how materials can carry memory through transformation.

Ceramic Installation, 2026

Taihu Stones and Foaming Glaze

In this project, I used physics experiments to develop specialized foaming glazes that reinterpret traditional Chinese aesthetics. When I added silicon carbide and calcite to the glazes, expanding gases produced cratered, porous surfaces on test tiles fired at 1180°C. These gases form bubbles within the molten glaze, leaving cavities as the glaze cools and solidifies. Realizing that these volcanic textures resembled porous Taihu stones of classical gardens, I sculpted abstract ceramic stones and coated them in foaming glaze. Staged on mirrors that evoke reflections in a garden pond, the installation connects material science with classical landscape design in a contemporary voice.

Experimental Ceramic Installation, 2026

Patterns Between Experiment and Imagination

During my residency in Jingdezhen, I conducted experimental glaze studies to explore connections between physics and ceramic art. I tested three dilution ratios of tobacco extract combined with eight raw mineral powders, applying the mixtures to sixteen 10 × 10 cm clay tiles fired at 1180°C. The resulting surfaces revealed branching lines, spreading colors, and clustered forms that reminded me of cells and cosmic structures. I paired these glaze experiments with AI-generated imagery, bringing material processes into dialogue with imagined worlds. Through this series, I wanted to explore how observation and imagination influence the way I interpret patterns.

Mixed Media Installation, 2026

Here Lies Anonymous

I created this site-specific installation to honor generations of unnamed ceramic artisans. At an abandoned Song dynasty kiln site near Bajiaojing in Jingdezhen, I placed a foam-board tombstone among discarded saggars, the containers that protected porcelain from ash and flames during firing. Like the artisans who used them, the saggars were left behind while the finished porcelain was valued. Beneath the inscription “Here Lies Anonymous,” I embedded a bisque piece, a decorated porcelain tile, and a saggar fragment. These materials represent different stages of making porcelain and commemorate the craftsmen whose skills and devotion gave porcelain its soul, though their names were rarely recorded.

Mixed Media Sculpture, 2026

What Holds Up the Palace

This sculpture examines how ancient ceramic artisans’ labor was exploited and hidden behind imperial splendor. I modeled the glazed roof after the Forbidden City’s Hall of Supreme Harmony, placing it on pillars made from color-glazed and blue-and-white porcelain vessels. A weathered wooden potter’s wheel supports the entire structure. The roof draws the eye with its grandeur, while accidental firing defects interrupt its polished appearance. By making the palace depend on the vessels and wheel beneath it, I wanted to honor the artisans whose work sustained imperial luxury, even as their names were left out of its history.

Mixed Media Installation, 2026

When We Shared the Same Screen

My childhood memories of Lunar New Year reunions inspired this mixed-media installation. Sharing potato chips with family while watching television gave me a sense of comfort and belonging. As we moved to different cities, those moments became less frequent. I hollowed out an old television and rebuilt a room inside it, using wooden furniture, PVC-printed backdrops, and ceramic chips and snack bags that I sculpted and glazed. The oversized snacks fill the small room, reflecting how an ordinary detail can grow larger in memory. Through the television frame, viewers look into a family gathering that I can revisit but cannot fully recreate.

Mixed media Installation, 2026

The Part of Me That Watches

This installation explores psychological dissociation and the distance I sometimes feel from my own emotions. When I am in pain, I imagine two versions of myself: one struggles with the feeling, while the other urges me to stop feeling and do something practical. I represented them as a realistic ceramic self-portrait head and a curled wax figure that slowly burns and melts. Red threads stretch between them, suggesting a barrier of willful blindness. In the video documenting the melting, the ceramic face appears unmoved. I wanted to show how trying to protect myself from pain can also leave me indifferent to it.

Mixed Media Sculpture, 2026

An Open Mind

This surrealist sculpture explores how physics and art shape my identity. My physics research has trained me to think through experiments, while painting and sculpture, which I have practiced since childhood, have given me space to imagine. I sculpted a clay self-portrait bust with an open head, using wires to extend light bulbs, a palette, brushes, and other objects beyond its surface. Although science and art begin on different sides, the wires cross and become tangled. Through this arrangement, I wanted to show how these two ways of thinking have jointly shaped me, even when they pull in different directions.
